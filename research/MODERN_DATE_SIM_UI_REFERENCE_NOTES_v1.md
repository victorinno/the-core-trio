# Modern Date Sim UI — Reference Notes v1

## Reference observations

### A Date with Death

The public game description frames its interaction as a romance **chat simulator** alongside apartment decoration, character customisation, and video calls. The takeaway for The Croe Trio is not to imitate a computer-chat theme; it is to give the player one believable, dominant interaction surface at a time. Romance, routine, and personal space should share a coherent UI grammar rather than competing HUD panels. [1]

### Our Life: Beginnings & Always

The public description emphasises a heavily choice-driven, customisable relationship experience, a changeable player identity, remembered preferences, and player control over relationship pace. Its relevant lesson is clarity and player trust: emotional choices should be readable, settings should feel supportive rather than technical, and relationship pacing should be communicated through language instead of exposed stat arithmetic. [2]

## Direct design implications for The Croe Trio

1. Replace the wide, decorative top rail with a small utility cluster that appears only when needed; player attention should stay on the scene.
2. Replace large dark dashboard slabs with a compact **moment card** that gives one current objective, one relevant action, and an optional secondary route.
3. Keep the scene image visible at all times. Use a bottom-aligned transcript/response strip only for prose; use a separate narrow, centre-lower choice stack for decisions.
4. Make buttons soft rectangles with modest radius, strong internal spacing, a clear hover/focus state, and no ornamental borders, vertical rules, pseudo-meters, or competing accent colours.
5. Move economy, NPC, accessibility, and location tools into a single understated command drawer or a small context menu, rather than forcing every control into the same visible header.
