# Cinematic Prologue Design — The Croe Trio

| Field | Decision |
|---|---|
| Language | English throughout the prologue interface and narration. |
| Format | Seven fullscreen canvas scenes, each advanced by `Enter`/click; `S` skips to Week 1; `R` restarts from the title. |
| Narrative voice | Second person, restrained and sensory; no relationship is framed as owed or won. |
| Visual system | Existing apartment and portrait art foregrounds character continuity; three new atmospheric backgrounds carry time, solitude and the doorway reveal. |
| Transition | The final line, **“Morning,” Alice says**, lands on a hold. The player then chooses **“Step into the week”**. |

## Scene beat map

| Scene | Visual focus | English on-screen copy | Function |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wedding rings against an evening window | “Alice met you in high school. Inseparable in the way gravity is.” | Establishes the marriage without presenting it as a possession. |
| 2 | Dinner-table warmth and city lights | “When she found polyamory, something in her lit up. You said yes—not from need, but from love.” | Establishes an affirmative origin. |
| 3 | Empty apartment corridor, a distant bedroom light | “The second year arrived quietly. Some rooms began to hold stories you were never there to hear.” | Names distance without villainizing Alice or Adam. |
| 4 | Dawn couch, coffee and an unclaimed hour | “Then Pamela sat beside you. Silence became the safest thing in the room.” | Introduces the emotional centre through small, chosen attention. |
| 5 | Pamela and Jessica as a warm portrait triptych | “Jessica asked, ‘Room for one more?’ And somehow, it became your morning.” | Shows the trio’s routine and leaves desire unclaimed. |
| 6 | Alice at the threshold, Adam’s room behind her | “This morning, Alice woke in Adam’s bed and felt the exact shape of an absence.” | Restores Alice’s subjectivity and agency. |
| 7 | Living-room doorway and the three-person couch composition | “She sees you at the centre of a closeness that has changed. Jessica looks up. ‘Morning.’” | Ends at an open, playable tension rather than a verdict. |

## Interaction and accessibility contract

The prologue never auto-advances. Scene counters, the current scene title and controls remain visible at all times. A dedicated **Skip prologue** action has no narrative penalty and starts a clean Week 1 state. A player can replay the prologue from the title at any time. Each scene keeps the text block compact enough to read without scrolling on a standard desktop canvas.
